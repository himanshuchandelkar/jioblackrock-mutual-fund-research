## 5. Due-diligence checklist for investors

Before choosing any scheme, review:

1. **Objective and benchmark:** Does the mandate fit the goal and intended holding period?
2. **Riskometer and loss tolerance:** Can you withstand a substantial fall in value?
3. **Total costs:** Check the current Total Expense Ratio (TER), exit load and, for a fund-of-funds, underlying costs.
4. **Portfolio and concentration:** Review holdings, sectors, credit quality and issuer concentration.
5. **Performance context:** Compare 1-, 3- and 5-year returns where available, benchmark returns and category peers; use rolling periods where possible.
6. **Debt-fund risks:** Check duration, yield-to-maturity, credit quality and interest-rate sensitivity.
7. **Tax treatment:** Verify the current tax rules for the specific scheme and holding period.
8. **Liquidity and process:** Understand redemption timelines, settlement, ETF bid–ask spreads and tracking difference where relevant.
9. **Documents and updates:** Read the latest SID, KIM, factsheet and all addenda; scheme terms can change.
10. **Personal suitability:** Consider emergency savings, debt, goal horizon and asset allocation before investing.

## 6. How to make a fair comparison

A reproducible comparison should record the date and use consistent data:
- Scheme and plan (Direct or Regular)
- Option (Growth or IDCW)
- Benchmark and category
- 1-, 3- and 5-year annualised returns where available
- Volatility, maximum drawdown and rolling-return consistency
- Current TER and exit load
- Portfolio risk measures relevant to the category
- Source URL and date accessed

Do not infer a fund's quality from its NAV, launch popularity, brand name or a single recent return number.
